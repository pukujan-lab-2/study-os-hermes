A burning candle is a remarkable example of how simple objects can reveal complex scientific principles. When the wick is lit, the heat from the flame melts the surrounding wax, creating a small pool of liquid fuel. This liquid is then drawn upward through the wick by capillary action, where it vaporizes and ultimately combusts. It is important to note that the wax itself does not burn directly; rather, it is the vapor that sustains the flame. Furthermore, the rising hot air draws cooler air inward, which helps to keep the outer edge of the candle solid. This delicate balance between heat, fuel, and oxygen underscores the elegance of combustion and highlights the fascinating interplay of chemistry and physics in everyday life.
